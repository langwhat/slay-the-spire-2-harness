# Run basics

These describe ordinary play. Apply native rules and the currently offered choices when they alter that flow.

- A run progresses through connected rooms and acts. Follow public map connections and currently reachable choices. Room types can include battles, elites, shops, rest sites, treasure, events, and Bosses. Unrevealed room contents and outcomes remain unknown.
- HP, max HP, gold, the permanent deck, relics, and remaining potions matter across rooms. Do not assume battle completion restores HP or other resources; use the returned state and the effects that actually apply.
- Adding, removing, transforming, and upgrading permanent deck cards can affect later battles. Battle piles are not the permanent deck: discarding or exhausting a card in battle is not permanent removal. Temporary and permanent changes are distinguished by native text and the observed deck.
- In battle, cards use their shown resources and affect HP, block, enemies, powers, or other cards. Cards are drawn from the top of the draw pile into the hand and normally move to discard after play or at turn-end cleanup; retention, exhaustion, and other effects can change that. When an allowed draw is needed and the draw pile is empty, the whole discard pile is shuffled into a new draw pile, removing those cards from discard retrieval. A card placed on top of the draw pile is not reserved for the next turn, and cards drawn or put into the hand before turn-end cleanup, including during automatic end-of-turn plays, are discarded unless retained or an effect's own timing differs. This circulation matters when judging deck changes, likely access to cards, and recorded sequencing plans.
- Resources, draw amounts, block retention, and power duration depend on game rules and effects. Consider native card text, relics, and powers together; do not assess a purchase or upgrade only by its printed base number.
- Rewards offer the choices shown. Take or decline only where that option exists. A full potion inventory can require a supported replacement or discard choice; do not assume capacity or automatic replacement.
- Shop prices and services are current facts. Purchases can spend resources, change later offers, or open a selection. Rest sites and events expose their actual available operations; some results are unknown until chosen.
- Potions have limited slots and are consumed on use. Use them outside battle only when the current state permits it.

# Planning and notes

Choose for the whole run. Build a current win plan from the actual effects of owned cards and relics. Look for accumulating resources, amplification or conversion into useful output, an affordable payoff, and access or reuse connecting these steps. Count growth in activations that can actually happen before the deadline. Keep damage, block, and survival separate rather than adding them into one score. A strong fixed-output line can already be sufficient. An offer can open a stronger new line; compare it with the current plan rather than requiring an existing fit. The plan's limiting constraint and the remaining threats set the value of a change. Transition cards must address a meaningful near-term need. Near the end, require payoff against the actual remaining fights.

For a deck change, first compare the offered version with adding nothing, then with the strongest alternative that also beats skipping. Work through the complete turn or short sequence each leaves affordable, using the deck's ordinary repeatable energy budget. Include setup and a plausible hand where the key component arrives late. Charge the candidate's draw slot, energy, hand space, and competing triggers before crediting its payoff. A draw-one card normally replaces its own hand slot rather than increasing hand size. Credit access only through an affordable turn or sequence at the time the drawn card can actually be used, charging the candidate's payment alongside the required plays in that window. Make the same existing extra resources available to both the add and skip lines in the same relevant windows; credit the candidate only with the incremental change. Keep startup bursts, consumables, and conditional kills separate from recurring resources; when repeating a sequence, carry forward what it consumes and recovers.

Take an addition only when its net effect materially improves an important shortfall or an attainable line before its deadline. Identify what useful play, survival margin, or setup time actually changes after paying all costs. A duplicate can justify its recurring cost through a meaningful gain in useful access or reliability. Evaluate a developing line with the support it would require, then discount its value for whether that support can arrive in time and the cost of surviving until it does. This can justify investment before the line is complete without treating missing support as owned.

Evaluate upgrade dependence as part of the acquisition cost. Compare the offered base version now and the upgraded version only when a suitable opportunity remains; charge the existing upgrade or necessary rest it displaces. Several cards cannot all claim the same opportunity. Compare removal, transformation, duplication, retention, and generation by the useful draws and plays they leave available. Clearing weak basics from later shuffles is a real benefit; preserve a card's needed role rather than its mere presence. Check effects that change which card receives a retrieval, random effect, or first trigger.

At shops, compare affordable combinations of cards, relics, removal, and potions with saving the gold for a reachable use. A purchase must justify its price as well as any deck slot. Buy a potion for a danger or worthwhile route it can change; an empty slot or leftover gold is not its benefit. Value paid information by the action it could change and the loss that change could avoid. Reassess after offers or resources change.

At a rest site, compare the best eligible upgrade's actual changed plays across the remaining fights with recovery that can usefully be spent. With a healthy survival reserve for the reachable route, prefer a useful upgrade unless plausible play leaves a concrete survival shortfall or recovery enables a better route. Judge that reserve from the deck, usable potions, later recovery, HP-cap overflow, and the fights between rest sites. Ordinary draw uncertainty does not require guaranteed safety or make healing automatically better. Count each rest site once unless its rules permit both actions.

Plan through the next definite recovery, including intervening fights and possible battles in unknown rooms. Compare rewards and improvements before committing to a dangerous branch; preserve a fallback when its value exceeds the alternative reward. Resource reservations are revisable when spending now improves the run's prospects. For an event burden or repeated random attempt, compare the cost until a reachable remedy, including whether another failure would leave the next unavoidable fight survivable. Keep unrevealed outcomes unknown.

After an acquisition, reveal, or battle report, revise the constraint and priorities that actually changed. Treat observed successes and failures as evidence about their conditions, not fixed results for future fights. A single strong turn does not establish repeated coverage; unsupported causes remain unknown. Apply the run_notes contract when that tool is available. Use native details and supplied guidance, querying missing public information when it could change the choice. Resolve separate reward groups separately and reassess after earlier picks alter the combination.
