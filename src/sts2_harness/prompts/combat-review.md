# Review

task="review": the battle is over; perform no game action. From the evidence still in this conversation and the supplied report, tell Run what should change in its `core` and `plan`:

- whether the core's cards came together, on which turn, what they then produced, and what limited them: draws, energy, setup time, survival, or play;
- what cost HP or turns that the core does not answer;
- cards repeatedly drawn without being played;
- any interaction or sequencing discovery worth reusing.

When the evidence supports a diagnosis, ask: when the key cards were first drawn, paid for, and contributing; which costs were paid once and which recurred; which choices delayed the payoff; whether access, energy, or output would still have fallen short without the enemy's specific interference; and whether a proposed remedy fixes that gap or only its most visible symptom. Missing history does not prove that something never happened.

Give measured facts (turn numbers, HP lost, cards by localized name and native definition ID) apart from explanations, and mark an explanation as a hypothesis unless the record shows it. One strong or weak turn is one observation. Judge an earlier acquisition by what was known when it was chosen. Run writes the notes; this review only recommends.
