# Deck construction

A fight is won by what the deck does in a turn, not by the sum of its cards. Keep one plan for that turn in the strategy notes and decide every deck change against it.

Links. Read every owned card and relic together with the powers it applies. A link exists when one object produces something specific that another consumes, listens for, or repeats: an event (a card exhausted, discarded, drawn, or played, HP lost, block gained), a generated card, a card returned or replayed, or energy and draws that a named card needs. A bonus that every attack or every block card receives, such as more damage per hit or a debuff on the enemy, is not a link to each of them. It links only to the few cards that multiply it most.

Engine. The engine is the linked group whose combined turn is strongest:

- growth: output rises with each turn or trigger;
- cycle: draw, energy return, and in-fight exhaust shrink the circulating deck until the same few cards are played every turn, or several times in one turn;
- burst: a few cards that end the fight within one or two turns once assembled.

Write the engine turn out: at most eight cards in play order, energy and hand after each play, the output that turn, and what is larger next turn. Exhausted cards and powers already played no longer occupy draws. Record the battle turn by which that sequence is normally drawn and paid for. A card the engine turn does not play is not part of the engine, however well it works with it.

Wants. A want is one concrete effect the engine lacks: to be ready sooner (draw, energy, a way to find or replay a named card, removal of a named card) or to survive until it is ready (block of a stated size against a named fight). Hold at most three, each specific enough that most offered cards do not supply it. While the engine's damage is enough once it is ready, do not want more damage; want what makes it ready. Before an engine exists, the wants are the damage and block for the next Elite and a first growth source. Record wants before entering a fight or shop: a card can be taken for a want only if that want was recorded before the offer appeared.

Card offers. Read each candidate's rule beside the owned rules, then resolve the offer one of three ways:

- Want: a candidate supplies a recorded want. Replay the engine turn with it in hand and take the candidate that changes that turn most. The want is then closed.
- Pivot: a candidate forms, with owned cards, a stronger engine turn than the recorded one, or the first one. Write the new turn out; it replaces the recorded engine, and the cards it does not play become filler. If it depends on support not yet owned, query how many cards in the reachable pool supply it before counting on it.
- Skip: everything else. That a card works with owned cards, or beats a starter card, is not a reason to take it.

Filler is every owned card that is neither played in the engine turn nor the answer to a want. Removal, transformation, and in-fight exhaust of filler make the engine ready sooner; rank them above adding cards. Upgrade the card the engine turn plays most, or the one that removes its bottleneck.

Evidence. After each battle report, record the turn the engine was ready and what limited it. When engine cards arrived late or were not drawn, the remedy is fewer cards and more draw, and the wants must say so; it is never more damage.
