# Deck construction

A fight is won by what the deck does in a turn, not by the sum of its cards. Keep one plan for that turn in the strategy notes and decide every deck change against it.

Links. Read every owned card and relic together with the powers it applies. A link exists when one object produces something specific that another consumes, listens for, or repeats: an event (a card exhausted, discarded, drawn, or played, HP lost, block gained), a generated card, a card returned or replayed, or energy and draws that a named card needs. A bonus that every attack or every block card receives, such as more damage per hit or a debuff on the enemy, is not a link to each of them. It links only to the few cards that multiply it most.

Engine. The engine is the linked group whose combined turn is strongest:

- growth: output rises with each turn or trigger;
- cycle: draw, energy return, and in-fight exhaust shrink the circulating deck until the same few cards are played every turn, or several times in one turn;
- burst: a few cards that end the fight within one or two turns once assembled.

Write the engine turn out: at most eight cards in play order, energy and hand after each play, the output that turn, and what is larger next turn. Exhausted cards and powers already played no longer occupy draws. Record the battle turn by which that sequence is normally drawn and paid for. A card the engine turn does not play is not part of the engine, however well it works with it. Record an engine only when both its growth source and the cards that turn it into damage are owned, and state the damage it deals on the turn it is ready and two turns later. Check those numbers against the HP and damage of the act's Boss and Elites as Deck shape describes. An engine that does not kill them in time leaves the deck short of damage, and an engine whose cost to its owner grows each turn must win before that cost does.

Wants. A want is one thing the deck is short of against the fights ahead, as Deck shape's two numbers show, stated as the kind of card that supplies it and the enemy number it answers: damage or growth while the kill turn is too late; block of a stated size while the HP lost is too high; draw, energy, or a way to find or replay a named card while the engine arrives late. Hold at most three, one for each shortage the numbers show, and none when they show none. A want is wide enough that a good card of that kind qualifies and narrow enough that a card of another kind does not. Before an engine exists, a first growth source is always a want. Record wants before entering a fight or shop: a card can be taken for a want only if that want was recorded before the offer appeared. Energy, draw, and block need no want; they are taken as support. After a take closes a want, record it again only if the numbers still show that shortage.

Card offers. Read each candidate's rule beside the owned rules, then resolve the offer one of four ways:

- Want: a candidate supplies a recorded want. Replay the engine turn with it in hand and take the candidate that changes that turn most. The want is then closed.
- Pivot: a candidate forms, with owned cards, a stronger engine turn than the recorded one, or the first one. Write the new turn out; it replaces the recorded engine, and the cards it neither plays nor needs as support become filler. If it depends on support not yet owned, query how many cards in the reachable pool supply it before counting on it.
- Support: a candidate gives energy, draw, or block, or prevents damage another way, and an engine or a growth source is owned. State what it buys: the turns it lets the deck survive against the listed intents, or the plays it adds to a turn, and the engine card that brings into play sooner. Take the candidate that buys most, unless the engine turn is already normally assembled and defended before the listed enemies' damage decides the fight.
- Skip: everything else. That a card works with owned cards is not by itself a reason to take it.

Filler is every owned card that is neither played in the engine turn, nor energy, draw, or block that supports it, nor the answer to a want. Removal, transformation, and in-fight exhaust of filler make the engine ready sooner; rank them above adding cards. Upgrade first the card whose upgrade most raises the engine's growth per turn or per trigger, then the one that removes its bottleneck.

Enemies. Keep Deck shape's result for each Elite and the Boss in `other`, one line each: what wins it, kill turn, HP lost, worst first turn. Redo a line only when the deck, relics, or potions change what it says.

Evidence. After each battle report, record the turn the engine was ready and what limited the fight, then set the wants from it. Engine cards arrived late or were not drawn: fewer cards and more draw, not more damage. The engine was ready and the fight still ran long: growth or damage. The fight ended on time and cost HP: block.
