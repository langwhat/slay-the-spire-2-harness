# Deck construction

A fight is won by what the deck does in a turn, not by the sum of its cards. A deck becomes strong when the cards it plays multiply each other.

Read every owned card and relic together with the powers it applies. For each, identify what it produces (energy, draws, block, damage, stacking stats, debuffs, generated cards, and events such as a card being exhausted, discarded, or played, HP being lost, or block being gained) and what it consumes, listens for, or is multiplied by. A link exists wherever one object's product feeds another's need. The engine is the linked group whose combined turn is strongest:

- growth: output rises with each turn or trigger, such as a stacking stat multiplied by several hits or a payoff per event;
- cycle: draw, energy return, and in-fight exhaust shrink the circulating deck until the same few strong cards are played every turn, or several times in one turn;
- burst: a few cards that end the fight within one or two turns once assembled.

Work out the engine turn concretely: the cards in play order, energy and hand after each play, the output that turn, and what is larger next turn. Exhausted cards and powers already played no longer occupy draws. Note the turn by which that sequence is normally drawn and paid for. A want is a concrete effect the engine lacks to be ready sooner, or to survive until it is ready in the fights that threaten the run; hold at most three, and one card that answers a want closes it. Filler is every owned card that is neither part of the engine nor the answer to a want. Before an engine exists, the wants are enough plain damage and block for the next fights and the first linked pair worth building on. Record the result in the strategy notes.

For each offered card, read its rule beside the owned rules and list its links in both directions. Replay the engine turn with the card in hand: which play it enables or replaces, and how the output changes that turn and across the fight. The card is an engine piece if it adds a link, more triggers, or earlier assembly; a pivot if it forms, with owned cards, a stronger engine than the current one whose turn you can write out; an answer if it closes an open want; otherwise filler. If a pivot or a want depends on support not yet owned, query how many cards in the reachable pool supply it before counting on it. Prefer the strongest engine piece, pivot, or answer. Filler must pass Deck arithmetic on its own numbers.

Removal, transformation, and in-fight exhaust of filler make the engine ready sooner; rank them alongside engine pieces. Upgrade the card the engine turn plays most, or the one that removes its bottleneck.
