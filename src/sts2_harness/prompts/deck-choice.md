## Making the card choice

Compare all available reward groups as one decision. Cards across different groups can complete a link if they can be taken together. Compare the resulting deck against taking a smaller subset of those additions and against skipping all of them, and default to skipping additions that fail this comparison. Judge every added card by what it replaces in the critical first deck cycle: before ordinary hands spend turn energy on chosen cards and before a real core exists, a strong low-cost card replaces basic starting plays and can lower a heavy `circulating_avg_energy`; once a real core exists or ordinary hands already spend turn energy on chosen cards, every non-essential card—including exhausting cards, powers, or low-impact draw-1 cards—delays key pieces before the first reshuffle, and skipping preserves draw density.

Before calling `run_deck_choice`, check in order:

1. **Current bottleneck(s)** (from `deck`, `deck_stats`, `fights`, `progress.route`, and Boss/Elite rules, before reading candidates):
   - Before ordinary hands spend turn energy on chosen cards and before a real core exists, check which basic demands remain uncovered—frontload/multi-target for rooms ahead, repeatable block, smooth energy curve, or this act's Boss output—and count how many **circulating** non-starter cards cover each job. Do not skip a strong card that plugs a genuinely uncovered basic demand just because another gap (such as Boss growth) is listed first.
   - Once a real core exists or ordinary hands already spend turn energy on chosen cards, narrow to the **single sharpest bottleneck** that most threatens upcoming Elites and Bosses (missing Boss ceiling, slow or over-costed hands, or short peak-turn mitigation), and do not treat secondary wish-list items as grounds to add cards.
2. **Link or clear upgrade** (for each candidate): Does its text form or multiply a real link with pieces you own or can take in the same plan, or directly close step 1's bottleneck? If it is a standalone card duplicating a job already reliably drawn in ordinary hands (a single exhausting card does not cover repeated turns), does it clearly beat your **best owned card** in that job and get played ahead of existing chosen cards in an ordinary hand? If a card needs to share a turn with a specific owned card without retain or fetch, separate **first-cycle reliability** (full `deck_stats.cards`, which governs hallway fights and opening turns) from **post-setup ceiling** (`deck_stats.circulating` plus active draw in long Boss and Elite fights): do not rely on it for short-fight survival when `cards` is thick, and only price it as Boss ceiling when a thin `circulating` pile or strong draw lets the pair recur once powers and exhaust cards are out.
3. **Weak grounds for taking** — lean strongly toward `skip` when the case for a candidate rests mainly on:
   - Beating basic starters while duplicating a job already reliably covered by stronger or multiple circulating owned cards;
   - Generic attack-wide buffs or enemy debuffs, minor play-count relics, class-wide tags or ordinary attack counts already supplied by common cards, or serving as plain exhaust or discard fodder;
   - Pre-upgraded flat numbers or modest extra utility on a card that still trails your core or duplicates a covered job;
   - Adding another copy of a non-core attack, setup, utility, or low-impact block card to compensate for a bloated deck—more mediocre circulating cards worsen draw consistency rather than solving it;
   - Adding expensive circulating cards (especially cost ≥ `base_energy` without block, energy refund, or cost reduction) into already energy-tight hands without energy help, or adding low-impact `0`-cost / self-copying filler without net draw or per-play scaling;
   - Relying on triggers you barely produce yet or adding redundant triggers without payoffs.

When taking a card after a core exists or once `deck_stats.circulating` is already high, briefly state in `reason` why its impact outweighs adding a draw slot and which owned card or specific bottleneck it surpasses.
