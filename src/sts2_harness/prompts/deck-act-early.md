## Stage threshold: first act

Hands begin mostly as basic starting attacks and blocks, and the immediate job is beating upcoming fights, Act 1 Elites on the route ahead, and this act's Boss without losing the HP needed to upgrade at campfires.

- Strong standalone frontload, multi-enemy reach, repeatable efficient block, and above-starter attacks or block that also draw 1 are worth adding early so ordinary hands have non-starter answers and Act 1 Elites pay out relics safely. A single exhausting block card covers only one spike turn rather than repeated attacks.
- Tighten each role as soon as it is covered—and tighten overall once ordinary hands spend turn energy on chosen cards or a real compounding core is in place: treat further flat-number cards in covered roles (including minor single-Boss tech or mediocre multi-target attacks) as competing against your best owned cards, and lean toward skipping unless they plug a still-empty Act 1 role, start a real growth link, or add net draw or energy. Do not keep adding redundant flat copies just to raise draw odds in an already growing Act 1 deck.
- Take early growth sources, payoffs with common partners, reliable energy, and net draw when offered—they appear less often than plain attacks, and holding two plausible starts early is normal. Do not skip a genuinely empty Act 1 survival or damage role for multiple floors waiting on a rare scaling piece.
- Respect your energy curve (`base_energy`): without energy or cost-reduction support, avoid stacking multiple heavy circulating attacks or utilities that crowd out blocking on the same turn (fix cost-heavy hands with energy or efficient 0–1 cost additions), and avoid taking a narrow payoff before an imminent Elite with zero owned triggers.
