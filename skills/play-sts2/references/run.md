## Game basics

These describe ordinary play. Apply native rules and the currently offered choices when they alter that flow.

- A run progresses through connected rooms and acts. Follow public map connections and currently reachable choices. Room types can include battles, elites, shops, rest sites, treasure, events, and Bosses. Unrevealed room contents and outcomes remain unknown.
- HP, max HP, gold, the permanent deck, relics, and remaining potions matter across rooms. Do not assume battle completion restores HP or other resources; use the returned state and the effects that actually apply.
- Adding, removing, transforming, and upgrading permanent deck cards can affect later battles. Battle piles are not the permanent deck: discarding or exhausting a card in battle is not permanent removal. Temporary and permanent changes are distinguished by native text and the observed deck.
- In battle, cards use their shown resources and affect HP, block, enemies, powers, or other cards. Cards are drawn into the hand, normally move to discard after play or at the turn transition, and may instead be retained, exhausted, or moved by effects. A draw first takes the remaining draw-pile cards; if further cards are needed after the pile empties, discard is shuffled to form a new draw pile and drawing continues if possible. This circulation matters when judging deck changes and likely access to cards.
- Resources, draw amounts, block retention, and power duration depend on game rules and effects. Consider native card text, relics, and powers together; do not assess a purchase or upgrade only by its printed base number.
- Rewards offer the choices shown. Take or decline only where that option exists. A full potion inventory can require a supported replacement or discard choice; do not assume capacity or automatic replacement.
- Shop prices and services are current facts. Purchases can spend resources, change later offers, or open a selection. Rest sites and events expose their actual available operations; some results are unknown until chosen.
- Potions have limited slots and are consumed on use. Use them outside battle only when the current state permits it.

## Strategy

For card rewards, default to skipping when the game permits it. Use current cards, relics, potions, and the visible route to assess how the deck wins, its damage and defense, the core plan's potential, setup, and sustained output. Pursue a feasible direction rather than several plans without shared support, but adapt to new resources instead of locking in early. Compare taking a card with keeping the deck, weighing upcoming challenges, long-term growth, energy and draw costs, marginal contribution, reliability, and route opportunities. Early on, favor cards that work well on their own or with modest support across several challenges; avoid repeatedly adding mediocre cards.

Account for how exhaustion, draw, and retrieval reshape the in-battle deck and its cycling, including repeated cycles within one turn or an infinite. Check required cards, setup time, energy, draw, survival before setup, and disruption from status cards. Basic starter attack and block cards are usually inefficient burdens, except for concrete synergies. A new card may dilute those cards or delay key cards; judge its net effect after draw, filtering, and card-search effects, not merely whether it beats a basic card.

Invest before a plan is complete when doing so does not seriously undermine near-term survival and development. Weigh a necessary stopgap's immediate benefit against its lasting burden. Do not assume missing components will arrive, or reject a card solely because its plan is incomplete. An upgrade can fundamentally change a card's value; assess whether reachable upgrade opportunities come in time, competing upgrades, and the possible need to rest, rather than assuming the upgrade will happen. Low cost, draw, rarity, or apparent synergy alone does not justify a pick. Compare visible options and legal combinations; skip when none justifies its costs.

Usually prioritize removing basic starter attack and block cards so efficient and key cards cycle sooner. Transforming them is very likely to yield a more valuable card. Weigh concrete synergies, the build, upcoming challenges, and the gold, HP, and opportunity costs of the available choices.

At shops, assess a card's contribution to the whole deck relative to not buying it. A discount is not evidence that the card belongs in the deck; use the actual price to check affordability. Compare buying nothing, smaller purchases, and combinations of cards, relics, potions, and services. Check both the whole combination and what each purchase adds, recalculating the remaining gold. Value saved gold against reachable later shops and known event requirements rather than a rule to spend everything or hoard it. Account for relic triggers and counters using the actual acquisition method. Recompute healing, gold, and thresholds when taking fewer cards or items, whether from shops or rewards; do not add a worse card merely to advance a counter.

Compare upgrades by their repeated value across upcoming fights: faster setup, earlier lethal, or attack and defense affordable together. Weigh this against healing and other upgrades. Healing can enable an elite route, preserve potions, or avoid another forced rest; a key upgrade may fix a deck problem that healing cannot. Compare survival under plausible poor draws, without resting for every theoretical worst case or counting uncertain future healing as guaranteed. Use unupgraded output when an upgrade cannot happen before the next threat. Budget preparation, dangerous fights, and recovery in route order; do not count a rest site as both healing and upgrading unless its rules allow both. Account for remaining special moves. Weigh elite rewards and consecutive losses against current strength, potions, and the Boss ahead, without fixed HP thresholds or elite counts. Before committing at a route fork, compare which fallback paths and recovery remain reachable if the next fight goes badly. When rewards are similar, value flexibility without automatically choosing the safest route. Reassess after substantial gains, losses, or spending.

Earlier steps can change the resources and options available to later ones. Combine supported actions and anticipated selections according to run_act's rules for references, random reveals, execution, and recovery.

After battle, distinguish enemy damage, self-inflicted HP loss, and healing; net HP change does not measure enemy damage. Use concrete performance and visible evidence to identify deck, route, or play problems, including avoidable card-play or potion mistakes. Decide whether to adjust in light of upcoming challenges, and leave causes unknown when evidence is insufficient. Retain conclusions that affect later decisions.

Use run_notes for durable plans and lessons that should change later decisions. Keep notes current and distinguish observations from hypotheses. Record potion reservations as revisable plans, not prohibitions; reconsider them when immediate death or a greater loss along the route is at stake. Identify game objects by localized name and native definition ID, with distinguishing properties when relevant, rather than relying on short references carrying across contexts.
